* Beta — conditions and caveats

Check the OBC table before passage

This page is a convenience, not an authority. Always cross-check against the OBC Bridge Gap Calculation Chart before you commit to a passage. Use at your own risk.

How the tide height is worked out

LINZ publishes four tide times a day, not a continuous curve. Every height between them is interpolated using the traditional maritime Rule of Twelfths: across the interval between one tide and the next, the water moves one twelfth of the range in the first sixth of the interval, then two, three, three, two and one. The OBC chart states that it uses the same method, and this page is checked against all 407 values of that chart.

The Rule of Twelfths is an approximation of a real tide, and it assumes a smooth interval between two published points. Treat any figure between the published tide times as an estimate, and the closer you are to the margin the more that matters.

What the figures do and do not include

The gap is the span height above chart datum less the predicted tide: 6.2 m for the IN/OUT span, 6.5 m for the HIGH span. Spare is that gap less your boat height and your safety margin. Nothing here accounts for your boat's trim, its load, or anything that moves in a swell.

A window is shown as safe only where at least 0.50 m of spare remains, caution where the boat fits with less than that, and danger where it does not fit at all.

Weather moves the water

Predictions are astronomical. Barometric pressure, wind and storm surge routinely shift the real water level by a tenth of a metre or more, and a low pressure system raises it. Add margin in adverse conditions.

Where the data comes from

Tide predictions are published by Land Information New Zealand for the Port of Auckland. They ship with this page, so it works without a connection once loaded. Times are New Zealand local time, including daylight saving.
